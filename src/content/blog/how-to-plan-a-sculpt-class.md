Knowing how to plan a sculpt class is what separates a class that feels sweaty and random from one that people rebook on the way out. The good news: most great sculpt classes follow the same simple structure. Once you have a reliable sculpt class template, planning becomes a 20-minute job, not a Sunday-night spiral.

Below is the 45-minute sculpt class format we see work again and again, broken down segment by segment, with timings, tempo ranges and movement ideas. There's a [free printable sculpt class plan](/downloads/sculpt-class-plan-template.pdf) at the end so you can fill it in by hand.

## What a sculpt class plan needs to do

A sculpt class mixes low-impact strength work with music: bodyweight, light dumbbells, loop bands, a ball or a beam, moved to the beat. A good sculpt class plan has three jobs:

- **Work the whole body** without hammering one area for too long.
- **Follow the music**, so every section has a clear tempo and count.
- **Build and release energy**, with a warm-up, a peak and a proper cool-down.

If your plan does those three things, the details (which squat, which press) are yours to play with.

## The 45-minute sculpt class template

Think in segments rather than minutes. One segment is roughly one song, usually three to four minutes, so a 45-minute class is about 11 to 13 songs.

| Time | Segment | Tempo guide | Movement ideas |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0:00–5:00 | Warm-up | 100–115 BPM | Marching, squat to reach, arm circles, hip hinges |
| 5:00–12:00 | Lower body 1 | 115–128 BPM | Squats, reverse lunges, sumo pulses |
| 12:00–18:00 | Upper body 1 | 115–128 BPM | Shoulder press, bent-over row, bicep curl |
| 18:00–25:00 | Glutes and legs 2 | 120–130 BPM | Curtsy lunge, glute bridge, banded side steps |
| 25:00–31:00 | Upper body 2 | 120–130 BPM | Chest press, tricep kickback, lateral raise |
| 31:00–38:00 | Core | 110–125 BPM | Dead bug, plank taps, oblique twists, ball squeezes |
| 38:00–42:00 | Finisher | 125–135 BPM | Pulses and holds, double-time combinations |
| 42:00–45:00 | Cool-down | Under 100 BPM | Hamstring, quad, chest and hip stretches |

Treat the tempo column as a guide, not a rule. A slower song still works for a fast move if you count it in half time or double time (more on that in [matching sculpt movements to BPM](/blog/matching-sculpt-movements-to-bpm/)).

## 45-Minute Sculpt Class Sequence

The table above is the shape. This is one way to fill it, Segment by Segment, with Movements you can put straight into a Flow. Swap any name for one you already teach. The minutes match the template, and each Segment is still about one song.

| Time | Segment | Movements, in order |
|---|---|---|
| 0:00–5:00 | Warm-up | Hip Circles, Straight Arm Round the World, Low Lunge |
| 5:00–12:00 | Lower body 1 | Squat, Reverse Lunge, Plié Squat |
| 12:00–18:00 | Upper body 1 | Arnold Press, Bent Over Row, Bicep Curl |
| 18:00–25:00 | Glutes and legs 2 | Curtsy Lunge, Glute Bridge, Side-Lying Leg Lift |
| 25:00–31:00 | Upper body 2 | Lying Dumbbell Chest Press, Tricep Kickback, Lateral Raise |
| 31:00–38:00 | Core | Dead Bug, The Hundred, Chest Lift with Rotation |
| 38:00–42:00 | Finisher | Chest Press Hover, Triceps Hover, Bear Hold |
| 42:00–45:00 | Cool-down | Saw, Spine Twist, Figure Four Stretch |

Read it in teaching order. That order is the class.

1. **Warm-up.** Stay standing. Hip Circles and Straight Arm Round the World wake the hips and shoulders up, then a Low Lunge rehearses the lunge you will load later.
2. **Lower body 1.** Squat, then Reverse Lunge, then Plié Squat. Big ranges first, pulses only at the end of the song if the room still has form.
3. **Upper body 1.** Arnold Press, then Bent Over Row, then Bicep Curl. The press has a pull in the same Segment.
4. **Glutes and legs 2.** Curtsy Lunge changes the plane, Glute Bridge takes it to the mat, Side-Lying Leg Lift finishes on the side. Do the side-lying work right, then left, before you roll on.
5. **Upper body 2.** Lying Dumbbell Chest Press, Tricep Kickback, Lateral Raise. Lighter than it looks. By now fatigue is doing some of the work.
6. **Core.** Dead Bug, The Hundred, Chest Lift with Rotation. Front of the body, then a little rotation. This is not a crunch marathon.
7. **Finisher.** Chest Press Hover, Triceps Hover, Bear Hold. Nothing new, and you are already on the floor. Short range, higher energy.
8. **Cool-down.** Saw and Spine Twist seated, Figure Four Stretch on your back. One clear closing line, then stop.

## Segment by segment: how to structure a sculpt class

The headings below are how to teach each part of that shape, whichever Movements you put in the sequence above.

### Warm-up (one or two songs)

Start standing, start simple. Big, easy movements that rehearse what's coming: a squat to reach previews your lower-body work, arm circles prepare the shoulders. Keep the count steady and cue posture early, because what you set up here carries through the whole class.

### Lower body

Open the working part of class with the biggest muscles. Build each song in layers: full-range reps in single time, then half-time reps with a hold, then pulses to finish. Layering gives every level an option without changing the move.

### Upper body

Switch to the upper body while the legs recover. Pair a push with a pull (a press with a row) so the front and back of the body share the work. This is where light dumbbells earn their place.

### Second lower body and glutes

Come back to the legs from a new angle: lateral lunges, curtsy lunges, bridges, banded work. Changing the plane keeps it fresh and spreads the load.

### Second upper body

Hit the muscles the first upper-body section didn't: chest, triceps, the sides of the shoulders. Keep the weights light; by now fatigue does the work.

### Core

Move to the mat. Core is a natural low point in tempo, which gives the room a breather while the work stays honest. Mix front-of-body work (dead bugs, crunches) with rotation and back-body work so it isn't 7 minutes of crunches.

### Finisher

One song, highest energy. Short-range pulses, holds and double-time combinations. Keep the moves familiar, since this is no moment to teach something new.

### Cool-down

Bring the tempo right down and stretch what you worked: hamstrings, quads, hips, chest, shoulders. End on one clear closing line, so class finishes on purpose rather than fizzling out.

## Building the playlist around your sculpt class structure

Plan the music and the moves together. Many teachers build the playlist first and write each segment to fit its song; others plan movements and then find tracks at the right tempo. Either works, as long as every segment has a song with a clear beat.

A few playlist rules that keep a sculpt class format running smoothly:

- **Match energy to the segment.** Warm-up and cool-down tracks should feel easier than the finisher.
- **Avoid long intros.** A 30-second intro is 30 seconds of standing around.
- **Check the beat before class.** Count a few bars of every track so you know whether you'll teach it in single, half or double time.

## How to balance a sculpt class so nothing gets overworked

The most common planning mistake is accidental overload: squats in the warm-up, squats in the first section, lunges in the second, squats again in the finisher. Each looks fine alone; together the legs are doing most of the class.

Before you teach, read your plan top to bottom and ask:

1. Does each major area (legs, glutes, arms, shoulders, back, chest, core) get a turn?
2. Is any area worked in more than two sections back to back?
3. Does every push have a pull somewhere nearby?

## Check the sequence on the Intensity Chart

Each Movement is scored from 0 to 5 on seven body parts: core, glutes, legs, arms, shoulders, back and chest. The Intensity Chart adds those scores across every Movement in the Flow. A Movement you use twice is counted twice. If you have not scored a Movement yet, the app estimates from its body area and says so on the Movement page. The bars are running totals, not a percentage and not one score for the whole class.

A part is flagged only when all three of these are true:

1. The Flow has at least 2 Movements.
2. That part’s total is at least 8 points.
3. That part is at least 40% of the sum of all the body-part totals.

The warning reads “Caution: possible glutes overwork”, with the part name in lowercase. Until all three are true, a high bar on its own is not a caution.

When a part is flagged, the chart lists Counter Movements: up to three options that work the counterpart and rest the overloaded part. Core pairs with back, back with core, chest with back, shoulders with back, arms with legs, legs with arms, and glutes with core.

The Positions card is the other half of the check. It lists the path of starting positions (Stand, Seated, On back, On front, Kneel, All fours, Plank, Side-lying) and counts how often the class changes between standing and not standing. That number is the up/down from the floor. If it is greater than 3, the card turns caution-coloured and adds: “Caution: lots of getting up and down. Try grouping standing work together.”

On the sequence above, that means teaching the standing Segments together, then staying down for the bridge, the side-lying work, the chest press and the core, instead of standing the room up between every song.

## Adapting the sculpt class template for 30 or 60 minutes

**30-minute sculpt class:** keep the warm-up, one lower-body section, one upper-body section, core, a short finisher and the cool-down. Around 8 songs.

**60-minute sculpt class:** add a third strength section (a full-body combination works well) and a longer cool-down. Around 15 to 17 songs.

The shape stays the same: warm up, build, peak, release.

## Sculpt class planning: quick answers

**How many songs are in a 45-minute sculpt class?** Usually 11 to 13, depending on track length.

**What BPM is best for a sculpt class?** Most working sections sit roughly between 115 and 130 BPM, with slower tracks for the warm-up, core and cool-down.

**How long does it take to plan a sculpt class?** Once you have a template, a new class usually takes well under an hour, and far less when you remix an old plan.
