Most barre classes follow the same order: a standing warm-up in the centre of the room, upper body with light weights, thighs at the barre, seat (glutes) at the barre, core on the mat, and a stretch to finish. In a 45-minute class we give those sections roughly 5, 7, 8, 10, 9 and 6 minutes. Each Movement inside them is layered the same way: full range, then smaller range, then pulses, then a hold, then a stretch.

That is the short answer to how to structure a barre class. The rest of this post is the long one: why the order works, how long each Movement should last on the music, and a complete 45-minute barre class plan with counts and cues.

## The order of a barre class

Look at how studios describe their own classes and the same shape keeps appearing. BarreConcept, a UK barre instructor certification, [lays out its class](https://barreconcept.co.uk/how-is-a-barreconcept-class-structured/) as a warm-up in the centre, at least half the lesson at the barre focused on legs and seat, centre work with light hand weights, floor work for the abdominals, then final stretches. Kalon Barre's [cardio barre class](https://www.kalonbarre.com/post/what-is-cardio-barre) runs an hour: about 5 minutes of warm-up, 10 minutes of arms, the bulk at the barre for legs and glutes, then 10 minutes on the mat for abs and glutes before a stretch.

Where studios differ is whether arms come before or after the barre: BarreConcept puts the hand weights after the barre work, Kalon Barre before it. We put arms first, for two reasons. The room stays standing between the warm-up and the barre, so there is no extra trip down to the floor and back. And the thighs and seat get a few minutes' rest from the warm-up before the longest part of class.

| Time | Section | Where | Songs | BPM (our guide) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0:00–5:00 | Warm-up | Centre | 1 | 115–124 |
| 5:00–12:00 | Arms, then push-ups | Centre, light weights | 2 | 118–126 |
| 12:00–20:00 | Thighs | At the barre | 2 | 124–130 |
| 20:00–30:00 | Seat | At the barre, then the mat | 3 | 120–128 |
| 30:00–39:00 | Core | Mat | 2 | 110–122 |
| 39:00–45:00 | Stretch | Mat, then standing | 1–2 | Under 100 |

For a reference point, IDEA's [sample barre-cardio-core class](https://www.ideafit.com/sample-class-barre-cardio-core/) uses 125–130 BPM for its warm-up and core, 130–140 for its cardio section and 100–120 for the cool-down. Our ranges sit a little lower because there is no cardio section here, and small-range work is easier to cue at a steadier tempo. Our [sculpt class music guide](/blog/sculpt-class-music-bpm/) explains how to choose and count tracks.

## How long each barre Movement should last

Barre Intensity's [class planning guide](https://www.barreintensity.com/blog/intense-talk/2017/6/19/barre-class-blueprint-instructor-training-planning-guide) gives the most useful number we have found: each Movement or variation should run for 45 to 60 seconds, which is 12 to 16 eight-counts. Anything two eight-counts or shorter counts as a transition.

The 12-to-16 figure assumes a tempo around 128 BPM. Here is how it changes:

| BPM | 12 eight-counts | 16 eight-counts |
|---|---|---|
| 120 | 48 s | 64 s |
| 124 | 46 s | 62 s |
| 128 | 45 s | 60 s |
| 132 | 44 s | 58 s |

### The 12-by-8 stack

We write every main barre Movement as the same 12 eight-counts. It is a version of the full range, then end-range pulses, then hold, then stretch sequence that BarreConcept describes, rounded to land on the music.

| Layer | Eight-counts | What it sounds like |
|---|---|---|
| 8 full-range reps, 2 counts down, 2 up | 4 | "Down, 2. Up, 2." |
| 16 half-range reps, one per beat | 2 | "Halfway. Down, up." |
| 16 small pulses at the hardest point | 2 | "An inch down, an inch up." |
| Hold | 2 | "Hold it still. Breathe." |
| 8 full-range reps, one per beat | 1 | "All the way, last 8." |
| Release or stretch | 1 | "Stand up, shake it out." |

At 124 BPM this takes 46 seconds. A three-and-a-half-minute song fits four stacks with a short transition between each. Repeat the stack and add a variation (heels lifted, a ball between the thighs, arms overhead) to stretch a Movement to 16 eight-counts.

## A 45-minute barre class plan

Equipment: a barre or a sturdy chair, a mat, the lightest pair of hand weights, and a small soft ball. Every main Movement below uses the 12-by-8 stack unless the counts say otherwise.

**Song 1. Warm-up, centre, about 120 BPM**

| Movement | Eight-counts | Cue |
|---|---|---|
| Step-touch with port de bras | 8 | "Step, touch. Arms open through second." |
| Second-position plié, 4 down 4 up, then 2 and 2, then single | 12 | "Wide stance, toes turned out. Knees bend over the toes." |
| Forward-tilt lunge, right then left | 12 | "Long spine forward. Back heel lifted." |
| Tendu front, side, back from first position, each leg | 8 | "Brush the floor away. Point, then flex." |
| Relevé roll-through | 6 | "Heels lift, knees bend, legs straighten, heels lower." |
| Plié to relevé, arms overhead | 8 | "Bend, then rise. Reach the ceiling." |

That is 54 eight-counts, about 3:36, plus a minute for welcome and set-up. Our [sculpt class warm-up](/blog/sculpt-class-warm-up/) has more on what a warm-up needs to do.

**Songs 2 and 3. Arms, centre, light weights, about 122 BPM**

- Song 2: bicep curl, overhead press, lateral raise to a T, then a chest fly ("hug a big tree").
- Song 3: rear-shoulder fly in a hinge, tricep kickback in a hinge, then put the weights down for push-ups (knees or toes) and a plank hold.

For push-ups, we adapt AFAA's [inclusive depth cue](https://blog.afaa.com/group-fitness-cues): "Lower until your upper arms are parallel to the floor, then press back up." It gives everyone the same target, whatever their strength.

**Songs 4 and 5. Thighs at the barre, about 126 BPM**

- Song 4: parallel chair with heels lifted ("Sit back like there's a chair under you. Heels high."); the same chair with the ball squeezed between the thighs; then wide second plié with heels lifted. Finish with a standing quad stretch, 2 eight-counts each side.
- Song 5: barre lunge with the right foot back and back heel lifted, then the left; then first position, heels together and lifted, with a small plié. Stretch the fronts of the hips before moving on.

**Songs 6 to 8. Seat, about 124 BPM**

- Song 6 (right leg): hands on the barre, hinge forward, then a back leg extension in parallel, the same leg lifting on a diagonal, and a bent-knee lift to the side.
- Song 7 (left leg): the same three Movements, same counts.
- Song 8 (mat): glute bridge, then a single-leg bridge each side. Finish with a figure-four stretch.

Start the second side on a new song. The counts reset, both sides get the same work, and you never have to remember where the right leg finished.

**Songs 9 and 10. Core, mat, about 116 BPM**

- Song 9: curl-ups with the ball behind the lower back, then oblique twists on the ball, each side.
- Song 10: forearm plank with alternating knee taps, side plank each side (bottom knee down as the first option), then back extension lying face down to balance all the front-body work.

**Songs 11 and 12. Stretch, under 100 BPM**

Hamstrings, hips, a seated twist, chest and shoulders, then a standing roll-up. End on one clear closing line rather than letting the music fade.

## How to program a barre class

This is the planning order we use:

1. **Choose the props first.** Barre Intensity's advice is to keep it to no more than two props per class, because the props shape the choreography. Weights and a ball is plenty.
2. **Decide whether you plan to music or to time.** Some instructors write each section to the length of a song. Others write to a clock and let the music play underneath. Either works; mixing the two in one class is where people lose their place.
3. **Pick two or three main Movements per section,** then build variations from them. In the thigh section above, a parallel chair becomes a chair with a ball, then a wide plié. That is three stacks from one idea.
4. **Teach both sides the same way.** Same counts, same layers, same song length.
5. **Check the balance before you teach.** Barre is leg-heavy by design. Make sure the back of the shoulders, the upper back and the back extensors all get some work, not only the fronts of the thighs and the abdominals.

It flags any area that is doing too much and suggests a counter Movement, which is useful in a class that spends 18 minutes on legs. ## Barre class plans for 30, 50 and 60 minutes

**30 minutes:** warm-up (1 song), arms (1), thighs (1), seat (2, one per side), core (1), stretch (1). Seven songs.

**50 minutes:** the 45-minute plan, plus a third thigh song with a standing pulse series in first position.

**60 minutes:** add a second arms song after the barre, as BarreConcept does, and lengthen core and stretch by one song each. About 15 songs.

## Barre class planning: quick answers

**How do you structure a barre class?** Most barre classes run in this order: a standing warm-up in the centre, upper body with light weights, thighs at the barre, seat (glutes) at the barre, core on the mat, then a stretch. In a 45-minute class that is roughly 5, 7, 8, 10, 9 and 6 minutes.

**How do you program a barre class?** Choose one or two props, decide whether you plan to music or to time, then write each section as a few main Movements, each layered from full range to small pulses to a hold, then a stretch. Teach both sides with the same counts, and check that the front and back of the body both get work.

**How long is a barre class?** Usually 45 to 60 minutes. ACE's guide to [creating a class blueprint](https://www.acefitness.org/continuing-education/certified/gfi-special-issue-2017/6453/creating-a-class-blueprint-elevate-the-experience-through-proper-planning/) notes that most group fitness classes last 45 to 60 minutes including the warm-up and cool-down. [Pure Barre's class formats](https://www.purebarre.com/class-formats) run 45 or 50 minutes, with a 30-minute intro class.

**How many times a week should you do a barre class?** There is no single right number. For general health, the [CDC recommends](https://www.cdc.gov/physical-activity-basics/guidelines/adults.html) muscle-strengthening activity on at least 2 days a week that works the legs, hips, back, abdomen, chest, shoulders and arms. A full-body barre class can count towards that.

**How long should each barre exercise last?** Barre Intensity suggests 45 to 60 seconds per Movement or variation, which is 12 to 16 eight-counts at about 128 BPM. Anything two eight-counts or shorter is a transition.
