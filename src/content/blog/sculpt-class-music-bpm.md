Sculpt class music works when each segment has a clear tempo and a song long enough to finish the work. Get the BPM right, and the room stays with you. Get it wrong, and even a well-planned sequence feels rushed or sluggish.

This is a practical BPM guide for building a sculpt playlist: tempo ranges by segment, how long each song should be, and two simple ways to put the playlist together. It sits alongside our [45-minute sculpt class template](/blog/how-to-plan-a-sculpt-class/) and the closer look at [matching sculpt movements to BPM](/blog/matching-sculpt-movements-to-bpm/).

## What BPM is best for a sculpt class?

Most working sculpt tracks sit between 115 and 130 BPM. The full playlist is a little wider: the warm-up often starts around 100 to 115, the finisher can climb to about 135, and the cool-down drops under 100.

That range is the go-to for sculpt, and it also covers what teachers look up as yoga sculpt BPM. You do not need a new song for every speed: change the count to half time, single time or double time, and one song can carry a heavy squat and a pulse in the same segment.

## BPM for every segment of class

Use this as a starting point for a 45-minute class, then adjust to the room and the music you actually have. One segment is roughly one song.

| Time | Segment | Tempo guide | Song length | What the music should feel like |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0:00–5:00 | Warm-up | 100–115 BPM | 4–5 minutes (one or two tracks) | Steady and clear, easy to march and reach to |
| 5:00–12:00 | Lower body 1 | 115–128 BPM | two songs, about 3–4 minutes each | Solid beat for squats, lunges and early load |
| 12:00–18:00 | Upper body 1 | 115–128 BPM | one or two songs | Same pocket; a slightly brighter track helps the energy hold after the legs |
| 18:00–25:00 | Glutes and legs 2 | 120–130 BPM | two songs | A little more drive, still clean enough for curtsy lunges and bridges |
| 25:00–31:00 | Upper body 2 | 120–130 BPM | one or two songs | Keep the pace; this is not the peak yet |
| 31:00–38:00 | Core | 110–125 BPM | one or two songs | A touch slower, so the room can hear cues on the mat |
| 38:00–42:00 | Finisher | 125–135 BPM | one song, 3–4 minutes | Highest energy, short-range pulses and holds |
| 42:00–45:00 | Cool-down | Under 100 BPM | one song | Soft landing; stretch, not a fade-out mid-phrase |

Treat the numbers as a guide, not a rule. A 118 BPM track still works for a faster move in double time, and a 128 BPM track can carry a loaded press in half time. The playlist sets the energy; the count sets the work.

## Song lengths and how many tracks you need

A 45-minute sculpt playlist is usually 11 to 13 songs. A 30-minute class is around 8; a 60-minute class is around 15 to 17. Plan song length to match the segment, not the other way around.

- **Three to four minutes** is the go-to length for a strength segment: long enough to layer full-range reps, a hold, then pulses.
- **Stack two similar-tempo tracks** if a segment needs six or seven minutes and you only have shorter songs.
- **Shorter tracks (two to three minutes)** work well for a punchy finisher, as long as you are not teaching a new movement in that window.
- **Skip long intros with no beat.** Start the movement when the beat arrives, or pick a different track.

If you already have a favourite 32-count workout track, keep it. A clear, consistent beat matters more than hitting an exact workout music BPM.

## How to build the sculpt playlist

There are two reliable ways to do this. Use whichever gets you to a finished plan faster.

**Music first.** Build the playlist in tempo order, then write the movements to fit each song. This is often quicker, because you are not hunting for a 124 BPM track late at night. Listen once: confirm the beat is easy to hear, note the BPM, and mark any mid-song drop where you might change the count.

**Plan first.** Write the Flow — movements sitting inside each segment, segments building the class — then find a track in the right tempo band for each one. This works well when you already know the work you want to teach and need the music to support it.

Either way, do a last listen with the plan in front of you:

1. Does the energy rise toward the finisher and come down for the cool-down?
2. Does every song have a beat you can cue to, even during a verse?
3. Can you finish the movement work inside the track, without rushing the last eight counts?

## A short note on playing music in class

A consumer streaming plan is not always enough for a group fitness class. Playing music in class usually needs a licence from the body that covers public performance in your country. The rules differ in the US, the UK, Australia and Singapore, so check the official guidance where you teach, or ask the studio which licence is already in place. This is not legal advice. ## Sculpt class music: quick answers

**What BPM is best for sculpt class music?** Working tracks usually sit between 115 and 130 BPM. Warm-up is a little slower, the finisher a little faster, and the cool-down drops under 100 BPM.

**How many songs are in a sculpt playlist?** A 45-minute class is usually 11 to 13 songs, one song per segment, each around three to four minutes.

**What BPM is yoga sculpt music?** Yoga sculpt playlists use a similar range to other sculpt class music: roughly 110 to 135 BPM for the working parts of class, with slower tracks at either end.

**Do I need a different song for every tempo?** No. Keep one track and change the count: half time, single time or double time, so the same song can carry a press, a pulse or a hold.
