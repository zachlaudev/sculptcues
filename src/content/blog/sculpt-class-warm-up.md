A sculpt class warm-up should take five to ten minutes, stay easy to moderate, and rehearse the Movements you are about to load: squat, hinge, lunge, press, row and plank, all at bodyweight. That is the whole job. ACE's guidance on [designing a group fitness class](https://www.acefitness.org/continuing-education/certified/gfi-special-issue-2017/6453/creating-a-class-blueprint-elevate-the-experience-through-proper-planning/) says the same thing in more formal language: a 5- to 10-minute, low-to-moderate dynamic warm-up through full ranges of motion, built from "rehearsal moves" that look like the main workout, only lighter.

Below is the warm-up we teach in a 45-minute sculpt class. It is two songs, ten Movements, and about six minutes, written in eight-counts so you can teach it on the beat. There is also a short section on swapping it for barre, mat Pilates and hot sculpt rooms.

## What a sculpt warm-up is for

A warm-up is not a smaller workout. If people are shaking by the end of song two, you have taught your first working Segment early.

We plan it around three things:

- **Rehearse the patterns.** Every Movement in the warm-up should show up later, loaded. A squat to reach now becomes a goblet squat in the first working Segment. A walk-out to plank now means nobody is meeting plank for the first time in core.
- **Bring the effort up gradually.** Start with marching and reaching, finish with lunges and plank. The second song should feel a little harder than the first, and both should feel easier than the first working song.
- **Read the room.** The warm-up is the best look you get at who is new, whose knees drift in on a squat, and who is already holding their breath. Note it. Those are the people you cue first when the weights come out.

## The two-song sculpt class warm-up

One eight-count at 108 BPM lasts about 4.4 seconds, so 40 eight-counts fill a three-minute song with a little room to spare. Song 2 is slightly faster. If your tracks run longer, add 8s to the Movements people find easiest rather than adding new Movements.

**Song 1: about 108 BPM, standing, no weights**

| Movement | Eight-counts | Count | Cue (say it 2 to 4 beats early) |
|---|---|---|---|
| March in place, arms swinging | 8 | Single time | "March it out. Swing the arms, long spine, soft knees." |
| Step-touch with overhead reach | 8 | Single time | "Step and reach. Reach the arm to the ceiling, then back down." |
| Squat to reach | 8 | 2 down, 2 up (16 reps) | "Sit back like there's a chair behind you. Stand and reach up." |
| Hip hinge, arms sweep back | 8 | 2 down, 2 up (16 reps) | "Soft knees, hips back, chest long. Sweep the arms back like you're closing a car door with your hips." |
| Squat, stand, hinge, stand | 8 | 2 counts each | "Squat. Stand. Hinge. Stand. Same rhythm." |

**Song 2: about 114 BPM, standing, then down to the mat and back up**

| Movement | Eight-counts | Count | Cue |
|---|---|---|---|
| Alternating reverse lunge, arms press overhead | 8 | 4 counts each side (16 lunges) | "Step back, lower down, press the arms up. Step in, stand tall." |
| Lateral lunge shift | 8 | 4 counts each side | "Wide stance. Shift right, sit into your right hip. Through centre, shift left." |
| Wide squat with rotation | 6 | 4 counts per reach | "Stay low, reach the right hand across. Back to centre. Left hand." |
| Standing row squeeze | 6 | 2 back, 2 release | "Elbows pull back, squeeze between the shoulder blades. Release." |
| Walk-out to plank | 8 | 16 counts per rep (4 reps) | "Fold, walk the hands out to plank. Push the floor away for 4. Walk back up." |

Use the last two eight-counts of song 2 for the transition: "Grab your lighter pair and bring them to the front of the mat." Your first working song starts with weights already in hand.

That is 40 eight-counts in song 1 (about 2:58 at 108 BPM) and 36 working eight-counts plus a short transition in song 2 (about 2:40 at 114 BPM). Call it six minutes, a little over the five-minute warm-up slot in our [45-minute sculpt class template](/blog/how-to-plan-a-sculpt-class/). Take the extra minute from the finisher if you need it.

## How long should a sculpt warm-up be?

Five to ten minutes. ACE puts a group class warm-up in that range, and for a 45-minute sculpt class we sit near the bottom of it, at two songs. A 60-minute class can take a third song: add a slow glute bridge series and a few cat-cows on the mat before the first standing Segment.

Shorten it and you start pushing the warm-up into the work. If you only have 30 minutes, keep both songs and cut a working Segment instead.

## Warm-up music: BPM and counting

Warm-up tracks for sculpt usually sit around 100 to 115 BPM. That range is set out in our [sculpt class music guide](/blog/sculpt-class-music-bpm/), and it is slow enough to talk over. Here is how long an eight-count lasts at common warm-up and working tempos, so you can check a song against the table above:

| BPM | One eight-count | 8 eight-counts | 40 eight-counts |
|---|---|---|---|
| 100 | 4.8 s | 38 s | 3:12 |
| 108 | 4.4 s | 36 s | 2:58 |
| 114 | 4.2 s | 34 s | 2:48 |
| 120 | 4.0 s | 32 s | 2:40 |
| 128 | 3.75 s | 30 s | 2:30 |

Two practical notes. First, the squat to reach and the hinge are written in half time (two counts down, two counts up). If your song is quicker than expected, keep the half-time count rather than speeding the Movement up; our guide to [matching sculpt movements to BPM](/blog/matching-sculpt-movements-to-bpm/) explains half, single and double time. Second, skip tracks with long beatless intros. Thirty seconds of standing around in the first minute of class feels much longer than it is.

If you teach with Sculpt Cues, Cue Coach listens to the song playing in the room, shows its BPM and pulses Up and Down on the beat at half, single or double time. It is useful here because the warm-up is where you set the count everyone will follow for the next 40 minutes.

## Barre class warm-up swaps

Barre warm-ups usually happen in the centre of the room, away from the barre, with ballet-based footwork. BarreConcept, a UK barre instructor certification, [describes its warm-up](https://barreconcept.co.uk/how-is-a-barreconcept-class-structured/) as knee raises, tendus and small pliés to upbeat music, with arm movements through the basic positions. IDEA's [sample barre-cardio-core class](https://www.ideafit.com/sample-class-barre-cardio-core/) gives 10 minutes to its warm-up: second-position pliés at changing tempos, port de bras, step-taps and lunges.

To turn the sculpt warm-up into a barre class warm-up:

| Sculpt warm-up | Barre swap | Cue |
|---|---|---|
| Squat to reach | Second-position plié with port de bras | "Wide stance, toes turned out. Bend the knees over the toes, arms open through second." |
| Hip hinge | Forward-tilt lunge, each side | "Step back, long spine tilts forward over the front thigh. Back heel lifted." |
| Reverse lunge | Tendu front, side and back from first position | "Brush the floor away. Point, then flex." |
| Lateral lunge shift | Step-tap side to side with arm circles | "Step, tap. Bigger arms on the second set." |
| Walk-out to plank | Relevé roll-through (heels lift, knees bend, legs straighten, heels lower) | "Up on the balls of the feet, bend, straighten, lower slowly." |

## Warm-up for a mat Pilates class

A mat Pilates warm-up usually starts on the floor. Keep the same principle (rehearse what is coming) and change the positions:

1. Lateral breathing, lying on your back: 4 to 6 slow breaths.
2. Pelvic tilts: 8 slow reps.
3. Knee folds or toe taps, alternating: 8 each side.
4. Bridge with a slow roll up and down: 6 reps.
5. Cat-cow on all fours: 6 reps, then a short plank hold before the main series.

Music matters less here, but if you use it, pick something under 110 BPM so breath and count do not fight each other. Our [sculpt vs Pilates guide](/blog/sculpt-vs-pilates/) covers how the two formats differ more broadly.

## Hot sculpt warm-up

Teach the same Movements, but make the warm-up feel easier than you would in an unheated room. Cut the walk-outs to plank to two reps, take longer to set up each transition, and tell people when there is a water window. CorePower's [first-class advice for Yoga Sculpt](https://www.corepoweryoga.com/blog/yoga-practice/yoga-sculpt-what-is-it-and-am-i-ready), which is taught in a heated room, is to take it slowly while the body adapts to the heat and to bring water and a towel. That is a good line to repeat out loud before song 1 starts.

## Common sculpt warm-up mistakes

- **Teaching new choreography.** The warm-up is for patterns people already know. Save the new combination for the second working Segment, once everyone is warm and following your count.
- **Loading the legs twice.** Forty squats in the warm-up, then squats in the first leg Segment, then lunges, then squat pulses in the finisher: each part looks fine on its own, but across the class the legs end up doing most of the work. Count squats and lunges across the whole Flow, not one song at a time.
- **Long static holds.** Holding a hamstring stretch for 30 seconds in minute two takes the pace out of class. Keep the warm-up moving and save the long holds for the cool-down.
- **Starting at finisher energy.** If your first track is your loudest, there is nowhere left to build to.

## Sculpt class warm-up: quick answers

**How long should a sculpt class warm-up be?** Five to ten minutes. ACE recommends a 5- to 10-minute, low-to-moderate intensity dynamic warm-up for group classes. In a 45-minute sculpt class we use two songs, which comes to about six minutes.

**What are 5 good warm-up exercises for a sculpt class?** March with arm swings, squat to reach, hip hinge with an arm sweep, alternating reverse lunge with an overhead press, and a walk-out to plank. Between them they rehearse the squat, hinge, lunge, press and plank you will load later in class.

**What is a warm sculpt class?** Usually a sculpt class taught in a heated room, often listed as hot or heated sculpt. The Movements are the same as a regular sculpt class; the room is warmer. Our guide to [what a sculpt class is](/blog/what-is-a-sculpt-class/) covers the formats.

**Do you need to warm up before Pilates or barre?** Yes. Mat Pilates and barre are lower impact than many classes, but the body still needs a few minutes of easier movement before the main work. In barre that usually means pliés, step-touches and arm work in the centre; in mat Pilates, breathing, pelvic tilts and spinal mobility on the floor.

**Should you use weights in a sculpt warm-up?** Usually not. Keep the warm-up at bodyweight so the room can find good positions first, then pick up the weights at the end of the second song, ready for the first working Segment.
