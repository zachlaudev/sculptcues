If you have ever searched for sculpt sequence ideas an hour before class, you know the gap: plenty of outlines, not many plans you can actually teach. You need the Movements, the counts, and a BPM that will carry them.

Here are four complete sequences — full body, arms and upper back, glutes and legs, and core — each written as two Segments (two songs, about six to eight minutes). They work in a music-driven sculpt class, and they also cover the usual yoga sculpt sequence ideas search: the same structure, light dumbbells, and counts that fit a 32-count phrase. Drop them into a [45-minute class](/blog/how-to-plan-a-sculpt-class/), or teach one as a focused add-on.

## How we write a sequence

A sequence is easier to teach when it has a job. In [ACE's guide to movement sequencing](https://www.acefitness.org/continuing-education/certified/october-2023/8462/movement-sequencing-strategies-to-build-a-smooth-and-effective-group-fitness-experience/), Lisa Bevers starts with the objective — a body focus, or whether the strength work should move from large muscle groups to small. Jan Schroeder, in the same piece, puts safety first: who is in the room, and what is realistic for them.

For the strength work, we follow a simple order. [NASM's guide to beginner strength routines](https://www.nasm.org/resource-center/blog/training/strength-training-routines-beginners) builds a session around five compound patterns first — squat, hinge, push, pull and carry — then adds accessory or single-joint work. That is the spine of the full-body sequence below. The arm sequence also follows a useful note from [ACE's research-backed workout](https://www.acefitness.org/continuing-education/certified/august-2023/8398/the-ultimate-ace-workout-an-evidence-based-approach-to-programming-and-class-design/): train the posterior deltoid first, then the front of the shoulder, because the back of the shoulder is typically the weaker of the two.

Counts stay in 8s. A 32-count phrase is usually enough for one idea: 8 reps in single time (down on 1, up on 2, so 16 counts), 8 pulses in double time, and an 8-count hold. Repeat the phrase for the length of the song, and use the last 8 counts to set up the next Movement. The BPM bands match our [sculpt class music guide](/blog/sculpt-class-music-bpm/). Light dumbbells and an optional loop band are enough for everything below. In the ACE sequencing article, Staci Alden recommends no more than three types of equipment besides a mat, and Bevers suggests using one prop for consecutive Movements so the transition can be the rest.

## How to read the tables

Each sequence is two Segments. Each row is one 32-count phrase: teach it, then repeat it. On the last phrase of the song, hold or walk it down instead of starting a new Movement. If you plan in Sculpt Cues, one Segment is one song, and the Movements sit inside it.

## 1. Full-body sequence

Use this when you want a balanced piece of work, or when planning time is short. Song 1 covers squat, hinge and pull. Song 2 covers a lunge and a press. Tempo: 115–128 BPM.

**Segment 1 — standing, light dumbbells optional**

| Phrase | Movement | Counts | Timing | Cue |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Squat | 8 reps (16 counts), 8 pulses, 8-count hold | Single, then double | Sit low, ribs over hips; keep the pulses small |
| 2 | Hip hinge to bent-over row | 8 hinges (16 counts), 8 rows, 8-count hold in the hinge | Single | Soft knees, long spine; pull the weights to the ribs |

Repeat phrases 1 and 2 to the end of the song.

**Segment 2 — standing, same weights**

| Phrase | Movement | Counts | Timing | Cue |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Reverse lunge, right, then shoulder press | 8 lunges (16 counts), 8 presses, 8-count hold | Single | Back knee lowers under the hip; press without flaring the ribs |
| 2 | Reverse lunge, left, then bent-over row | 8 lunges (16 counts), 8 rows, 8-count hold | Single | Same leg work on the second side; keep the row close to the body |

If the room is new to load, drop the weights for the lunges and keep them for the press and row.

## 2. Sculpt arm sequence (arms and upper back)

This is the sculpt arm sequence we reach for when the legs have already been worked, or when the plan is an upper-body focus. Posterior work comes first, then the press, then smaller arm work. Tempo: 120–130 BPM.

**Segment 1 — standing hinge and press**

| Phrase | Movement | Counts | Timing | Cue |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Bent-over row, then reverse fly | 8 rows (16 counts), 8 reverse flies, 8-count hold with the arms wide | Single | Soft knees; squeeze the shoulder blades before the arms move |
| 2 | Shoulder press, then pulse | 8 presses (16 counts), 8 pulses at the top, 8-count hold | Single, then double | Wrists stacked over elbows; ribs stay heavy |

**Segment 2 — smaller arm work**

| Phrase | Movement | Counts | Timing | Cue |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Bicep curl, then tricep kickback | 8 curls (16 counts), 8 kickbacks, 8-count hold in the kickback | Single | Elbows stay close; the hinge stays still for the kickbacks |
| 2 | Lateral raise, then pulse | 8 raises (16 counts), 8 pulses, 8-count hold | Single, then double | Lead with the elbows; stop at shoulder height |

ACE's back-exercise research in that same workout article found the bent-over row to be a strong option for the upper back, so it opens this sequence.

## 3. Glutes and legs sequence

This lower-body sequence starts with a bilateral squat, then moves to curtsy lunges and a mat glute series. Tempo: 120–130 BPM.

**Segment 1 — standing**

| Phrase | Movement | Counts | Timing | Cue |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Squat or sumo squat | 8 reps (16 counts), 8 pulses, 8-count hold | Single, then double | Heels stay heavy; knees track over the toes |
| 2 | Curtsy lunge, right then left | 8 right (16 counts), 8 left (16 counts) | Single | Hip, knee and toe point the same way; keep the chest lifted |

**Segment 2 — mat**

Use the last lunge of song 1 to lower the back knee and come to the floor, the way Alden describes a standing-to-mat transition in the ACE sequencing article.

| Phrase | Movement | Counts | Timing | Cue |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Glute bridge | 8 reps (16 counts), 8 pulses, 8-count hold | Single, then double | Ribs heavy, squeeze at the top; keep the pulses small |
| 2 | Single-leg bridge, right then left, or marching bridge | 8 right (16 counts), 8 left (16 counts) | Single | Hips stay level; option to keep both feet down and march |

## 4. Core sequence

Keep the tempo a little slower so the room can hear you. Tempo: 110–125 BPM. Alden, in the ACE article, asks where the spine is moving — forward, back and side to side — so this sequence uses a dead bug, a plank and a side plank, rather than a long string of crunches.

**Segment 1 — on the mat**

| Phrase | Movement | Counts | Timing | Cue |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Dead bug | 8 alternating reps (32 counts, 4 counts each) | Half time | Low back stays heavy; move the opposite arm and leg |
| 2 | Forearm plank with shoulder taps | 8-count plank, 8 taps, 8-count plank, 8-count rest on the knees | Single | Hips quiet; option to keep both forearms down and hold |

**Segment 2 — side plank, both sides**

| Phrase | Movement | Counts | Timing | Cue |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Side plank, right | 16-count hold, then 8 slow hip dips (16 counts) or a second 16-count hold | Single | Shoulder stacked over elbow; drop the bottom knee if you need it |
| 2 | Side plank, left, same shape | Same counts | Single | Match the first side, then walk it down to finish |

## A note on hot sculpt sequences

Yoga sculpt is often taught in a heated room, as [Lindsey Bomgren notes in her 30-minute yoga sculpt class](https://www.nourishmovelove.com/30-minute-yoga-sculpt/). A hot sculpt sequence does not need different Movements. Keep the same phrases, leave a longer pick-up between songs, offer an earlier option to drop the weights, and keep the floor transitions tidy — heat makes messy changes feel harder. Beginners can also slow the count or skip the pulses, the same kind of modification Lindsey suggests when the pace or the heat is a lot.

## Where these sequences sit in a 45-minute class

If you are teaching a full class, treat each sequence as two Segments in the [45-minute sculpt class template](/blog/how-to-plan-a-sculpt-class/). The full-body sequence can replace lower body 1 and upper body 1. The arm sequence fits upper body 1 or 2. The glutes and legs sequence fits the second lower-body section. The core sequence belongs in the core songs, before the finisher. One lower-body sequence, one arm sequence, then core, is already most of a balanced Flow. For half time, single time and double time on the same track, see [matching sculpt movements to BPM](/blog/matching-sculpt-movements-to-bpm/).

Once a sequence works, save it and teach it again. In Sculpt Cues you can build it as a Flow — Movements inside Segments, Segments building the Flow — and keep full-body, arms, glutes and core versions in separate folders. ## Sculpt sequence ideas: quick answers

**What are good yoga sculpt sequence ideas?** A repeating 32-count phrase with a clear objective. Start with full body (squat, hinge, push, pull), or teach a focused arm, glute or core sequence.

**How many reps should be in a sculpt sequence?** Work in 8s inside a 32-count phrase. Eight single-time reps, eight pulses and an eight-count hold is a go-to shape.

**Can I use these in a hot sculpt class?** Yes. Same Movements, slower transitions, an earlier option to drop the weights, and a clearer window for water.

**How do I add a sequence to a 45-minute class?** Two songs per sequence, dropped into the matching part of the template.
