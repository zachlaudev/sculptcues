A sculpt class is a coach-led, full-body strength workout that uses lighter resistance, higher reps and controlled timing to build muscular endurance. You will usually work to a playlist, with light dumbbells or another form of load, and move through a planned sequence that covers legs, upper body and core.

If you have been wondering what a sculpt class is, that is the short answer. The longer one matters, because "sculpt" is a category, not a single method. [Equinox describes a sculpt class](https://www.equinox.com/groupfitness/body-sculpt-class) as a full-body conditioning workout that builds strength and muscular endurance through controlled, low-impact movement with lighter weights. Look at a few studio timetables and you will also see yoga sculpt, barre sculpt, hot sculpt and reformer sculpt. They share a family resemblance, but the equipment and the room change how the class feels.

If you are choosing a first class, read the description, not only the name. If you are teaching, say plainly what people will actually do. Our [sculpt vs Pilates guide](/blog/sculpt-vs-pilates/) covers how sculpt differs from the Pilates method; this post is about the class itself.

## Is sculpt strength training?

Yes. A sculpt class is strength work, usually aimed at muscular endurance rather than a one-rep maximum. [Equinox's Body Sculpt](https://www.equinox.com/groupfitness/body-sculpt-class) is an endurance-based, low-impact workout that uses high reps and light-to-medium weights, often with bands or gliding discs. That programming sits comfortably with [ACE's training variables for muscular endurance](https://www.acefitness.org/continuing-education/certified/august-2024/8682/the-ace-workout-builder-for-circuit-training/): lighter loads (no more than about 67% of a one-rep maximum), at least 12 repetitions, and short rest.

You may also get a cardio lift, especially in yoga sculpt or a cardio sculpt format, because the transitions stay short and the playlist keeps you moving. That does not make it a jumping interval class, and it does not make it Pilates. It is loaded, often music-driven work that you can usually keep low-impact.

## Sculpt class formats

"Sculpt" on a timetable usually means one of these. None is the single correct version; they are different classes that share a name.

| Format | Typical equipment | How it usually feels | Good to know |
|---|---|---|---|
| Mat or body sculpt | Mat, light dumbbells, loop bands, sometimes a ball or gliders | Standing and floor strength work, often to a playlist | A common gym and boutique format, and a natural fit for a [planned 45-minute class](/blog/how-to-plan-a-sculpt-class/) |
| Barre sculpt | A barre or sturdy chair, light weights, bands, a small ball | Small-range reps and held positions, with a strong focus on legs, glutes and posture | [ACE's barre overview](https://www.acefitness.org/continuing-education/certified/december-2019/7432/hitting-the-barre-understanding-the-popular-group-fitness-trend/) describes barre work as high-rep, often isometric, with light dumbbells, balls and bands |
| Yoga sculpt | Mat and light hand weights, sometimes bands | A yoga-based flow with weighted strength work and, in some studios, cardio bursts | [CorePower Yoga](https://www.corepoweryoga.com/blog/yoga-practice/yoga-sculpt-what-is-it-and-am-i-ready) describes Yoga Sculpt as yoga postures with optional hand weights and cardio bursts; [Northwestern Recreation](https://nurecreation.com/sports/2015/8/12/groupxdescriptions.aspx) uses a mix of 3-, 5- and 8-lb weights inside a vinyasa flow |
| Hot sculpt | The same as the underlying format, in a heated room | The same Movements, with more sweat and a slower need to rush the transitions | Heat is a room condition, not a method. Some yoga sculpt classes are heated; many mat and gym sculpt classes are not |
| Reformer sculpt | Reformer springs, sometimes added weights | Loaded, usually low-impact work against spring resistance | The pace can be controlled or interval-based, depending on the studio |

For music-driven mat sculpt, working tracks often sit around 115 to 130 BPM. Our [sculpt class music guide](/blog/sculpt-class-music-bpm/) sets out the ranges by Segment.

## What equipment do you need?

For most mat and yoga sculpt classes, this is enough:

- A mat
- A light pair of dumbbells, and sometimes a slightly heavier pair
- A loop band
- Water, and a towel if the room is heated

[CorePower's Yoga Sculpt guide](https://www.corepoweryoga.com/blog/yoga-practice/yoga-sculpt-what-is-it-and-am-i-ready) notes that weights are optional, and that a lighter set is useful for the warm-up. Start lighter than you think in a first class. You can always pick up a heavier pair if the load feels easy and your form is solid.

Barre sculpt may add a barre, or the back of a chair, and a small ball. Reformer sculpt provides the machine; you usually do not bring your own springs. If the class description mentions gliders, ankle weights or a Pilates ball, the studio will often have them, but it is worth checking when you book.

## Are sculpt classes heated?

Not always. Some are; many are not.

[CorePower's Yoga Sculpt](https://www.corepoweryoga.com/yoga-fitness-classes/yoga-sculpt/studio) is practised in a heated room, and their [first-class advice](https://www.corepoweryoga.com/blog/yoga-practice/yoga-sculpt-what-is-it-and-am-i-ready) is to take it slow while you adapt to the heat, and to bring extra water and a towel. A mat sculpt class in a gym studio, or a barre sculpt class, may be taught at ordinary room temperature.

If heat matters to you, look for the words "hot", "heated" or a listed room temperature in the description. If you are writing that description as an instructor, say whether the room is heated. Students should not have to guess.

If you want the comparison in more depth, read [Hot sculpt vs hot Pilates](/blog/hot-sculpt-vs-hot-pilates/).

## What to expect in your first sculpt class

A typical 45- or 50-minute class moves through a warm-up, lower-body work, upper-body work, core, a short finisher and a cool-down. Instructors who plan in Segments — one song, one section of work — will change the focus as the playlist moves. Our [45-minute sculpt class template](/blog/how-to-plan-a-sculpt-class/) shows that shape in full.

You should expect:

- Coaching throughout, not a silent workout
- Options to make a Movement lighter or stronger
- Higher reps than a heavy lifting session, with pauses, pulses and holds
- A room that may or may not be heated, depending on the format

A simple first-class checklist:

1. Read the class description for format, heat and equipment.
2. Arrive a few minutes early so you can set up your mat and choose weights.
3. Start with the lighter dumbbells. Weights are often optional.
4. Bring water. Add a towel if the room is heated.
5. Take the modification, or rest, whenever you need it. Form matters more than keeping up with the person next to you.

CorePower encourages students to take Yoga Sculpt at their own pace and to rest whenever needed. That advice travels well to any sculpt format.

## Can you track a sculpt class on Apple Watch?

Yes, though Apple does not list a workout type called "Sculpt". In the [Workout app on Apple Watch](https://support.apple.com/en-us/105089), you can start Barre, Pilates, Yoga, Functional Strength Training, Traditional Strength Training, Core Training, or Other if nothing else fits. Pick the type that is closest to the class you booked, then start the workout from the Workout app before class begins, as [Apple's guide to starting a workout](https://support.apple.com/guide/watch/start-a-workout-apdd16e8761a/watchos) explains.

If you are the one teaching, Sculpt Cues has Apple Watch support of its own. The Flow you planned on your iPhone — Movements inside Segments, Segments building the class — can sit on your wrist, so you can see the current Segment, the Movements in it, and what comes next without going back to your phone. Cue Coach still picks up the BPM of whatever is playing in the room.

## For instructors

If you teach sculpt, barre or mat Pilates, the useful part of this definition is the one you put in the class description: format, equipment, heat, and what a first-timer should bring. Then plan the class as a Flow, so the promise in the description matches the work in the room.
